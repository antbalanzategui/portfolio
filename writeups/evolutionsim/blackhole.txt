BLACK HOLE — black_hole_cinematic_gpu
======================================

A real-time, GPU-ray-traced reconstruction of M87*, the 6.5 x 10^9 solar mass
supermassive black hole imaged by the Event Horizon Telescope in 2019. The
scene runs as a cinematic loop viewed at the EHT's 17 degree inclination with
spin a/M = 0.94. Every visible feature emerges from first-principles general
relativity — nothing is painted in.


THE SIMULATION, END TO END
--------------------------

Steady state (the persistent scene). A geometrically thin, optically thick
accretion disk orbits between the ISCO (innermost stable circular orbit) and
~10 R_g. The disk's signature features are all relativistic effects:

  - The bright crescent is Doppler beaming — the side of the disk rotating
    toward the camera is blueshifted and brightness-boosted by relativistic
    motion (delta-beta ~ 0.3 at ISCO); the receding side is dimmed and
    redshifted.

  - The Einstein ring surrounding the dark shadow is the photon sphere —
    light rays grazing R = 3 R_g loop the black hole one or more times before
    reaching the camera.

  - The secondary image visible *inside* the apparent shadow is the disk's
    far side, bent over the top of the hole by rays deflected by pi radians.

  - The dark center is the apparent shadow at 5.2 R_g — slightly D-shaped
    because the black hole is spinning.

Orbiting hot spots. Eight bright "hot spots" — small overdensities at ISCO —
orbit at 1.5–3x ISCO radius with Keplerian frequencies. They are gravitationally
lensed automatically by the per-pixel geodesic integration, so they appear,
brighten, lens around the back of the hole, and reappear on the far side
without any explicit animation.

MAD eruptions (Magnetically Arrested Disk flux eruptions; Igumenshchev 2008,
Tchekhovskoy 2011, Ripperda 2022). Stochastically launched plasma arcs erupt
from the disk along poloidal magnetic field lines, modeled as fat red-orange
tubes anchored at both ends. Maximum one active at a time — they are asymmetric
by nature.

Tidal Disruption Event. A gas cloud (modeled on Sgr A*'s G2 cloud, Gillessen
2012) approaches the black hole on a parabolic orbit. Inside the tidal radius
(~3x ISCO):

  - The cloud undergoes spaghettification — differential gravity stretches it
    along the radial direction, rendered as 24 sub-spheres tapering from a hot
    core to long thin extremities.

  - A debris stream unspools behind the disrupted star (Rees 1988), with
    non-linear lag from differential orbital velocities: dense near the head,
    sparse at the tail. The stream wraps around the hole and circularizes.

  - The tip facing the black hole is tidally compressed and heated, glowing
    hotter than the trailing material.

  - Debris that crosses the horizon disappears; everything else is
    gravitationally lensed by the ray-trace.

Gravitational-wave grid ripple — a hidden curvature grid driven by sin-product
waves models the spacetime distortion (an aesthetic overlay, not load-bearing
physics).

Camera choreography. Slow continuous orbit around the disk, with pitch /
distance shaped by the cinematic phase clock. The shot starts pulled back,
drifts inward through the MAD-eruption phase, then frames the TDE as it
disrupts.


PHYSICS ENGINE
--------------

  src/relativistic_physics.py — KerrBlackHole builds the full Kerr geometry
  (outer horizon R+, ISCO via Bardeen-Press-Teukolsky, photon sphere,
  ergosphere, Hawking temperature, Eddington luminosity) from just mass_solar
  and spin. AccretionDisk adds the Novikov-Thorne (1973) relativistic disk
  temperature profile. RelativisticJet handles Blandford-Znajek (1977) jet
  power from spin extraction.

  src/mad_eruptions.py — stochastic MAD eruption engine; samples eruption
  phase, brightness, and radial extent.

  Pseudo-Newtonian acceleration (Paczynski-Wiita) — a = -GM / (r - R_s)^2 —
  drives CPU-side particle dynamics (TDE debris, hot spots). It reproduces
  ISCO and horizon behavior without full geodesic cost per particle.


RENDERING PIPELINE
------------------

Three GPU passes per frame:

1. Per-pixel Kerr ray-trace (renderer/src/shaders/black_hole.wgsl). For every
   screen pixel, the GPU integrates a null geodesic *backward* from the camera
   through Kerr spacetime using RK4 integration in the equatorial plane with
   conserved energy E and angular momentum L = b (impact parameter). Rays that
   fall through the outer horizon return black. Rays grazing the photon sphere
   produce the bright ring. Rays deflected ~pi give the Einstein ring and
   secondary image. The shader handles disk emission with Doppler beaming and
   gravitational redshift inline.

2. Volume rendering of the disk's emission cube (200 x 60 x 200 grid baked
   once at startup with an exponential radial profile and Gaussian vertical
   falloff; density gated to clear the shadow region so glow cannot bleed
   inside 2.6 R_g).

3. Sphere/particle pipeline for hot-spots, MAD eruption plasma tubes, and TDE
   debris. These get gravitationally lensed naturally because the ray-trace
   handles the secondary crossing.

CPU packs ~256 bytes of state per frame (orbital phases, MAD state, TDE
position) and writes to a memory-mapped buffer; GPU reads and renders.
60 FPS on a GTX 1660 at the captured resolution.
