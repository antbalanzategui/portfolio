NEUTRON STAR MERGER — neutron_star_merger_cinematic_gpu
========================================================

A cinematic of two ~1.4 solar mass neutron stars spiraling in via
gravitational-wave emission, colliding, forming a hypermassive remnant, and
exploding into a kilonova — the same event LIGO and Virgo observed as GW170817
in August 2017. Parameters are pinned to the measured GW170817 values: chirp
mass, jet half-angle, ejecta velocities.


THE SIMULATION, END TO END
--------------------------

Phase 0 — Inspiral. Two compact objects on a decaying orbit. Separation
follows the Peters (1964) inspiral solution, a(t) proportional to
(1 - t/t_merge)^(1/4), with orbital frequency increasing as the characteristic
gravitational-wave chirp. Expanding ring patterns visualize the h+ / h-cross
strain pattern of quadrupole gravitational radiation, emitted steadily early
on and accelerating into a torrent at merger. The camera holds at an
overhead-ish angle, tightening as the stars approach.

Phase 1 — Merger. The EOS-dependent tidal-disruption threshold is
crossed. Tidal arms unspool from each star. Cores collide. A corona flash marks
contact — a 3-frame near-whiteout followed by a fast corona expansion to ~60
sim units. This is the optical/UV "blue precursor" the kilonova actually
shows.

Phase 2 — Post-merger. A hypermassive neutron star (HMNS) survives,
oscillating in multiple normal modes simultaneously, all damped with realistic
timescales from numerical-relativity simulations:

  - f2 mode — the dominant 2–3 kHz post-merger gravitational-wave peak
  - Spiral (m=1) mode — characteristic one-arm instability
  - Quasi-radial mode — radial breathing
  - One-arm mode — m=1 dynamical instability

A short-lived accretion disk forms around the remnant with MRI-driven spiral
density waves (magnetorotational instability) at the proper pitch angle. Twin
relativistic jets launch along the polar axis with a helical magnetic
structure, surrounding cocoon, ~3 degree precession period, and a luminous
breakout core. These jets are the engine that powers the short gamma-ray
burst.

Phase 3 — Kilonova. Two-component ejecta expansion — the same two
components observed in GW170817's optical/IR follow-up:

  - Red component: dynamical ejecta, equatorial, lanthanide-rich, high opacity.
    Thrown off during the merger itself.
  - Blue component: wind ejecta, polar, lanthanide-poor, lower opacity. Driven
    by neutrino winds from the disk.

Both shells are heated by r-process radioactive decay — the rapid neutron
capture nucleosynthesis that forges gold, platinum, and the actinides in real
time. The brightness decay follows the r-process heating-rate power law
t^(-1.3). The jets keep pushing through the expanding shell, visible as bright
filaments piercing both poles.

Phase 4 — Cycle reset. Smooth fade and re-seed; the loop restarts.

Camera choreography. Overhead during inspiral, side-on for the merger, slow
orbit during post-merger, then a slow pullback during the kilonova to frame
the full jet + ejecta geometry.


PHYSICS ENGINE
--------------

  - Peters-Mathews (1964) gravitational-wave inspiral.

  - Chirp mass and orbital parameters taken from GW170817's measured values
    via src/physics_constants.py (NSMERGER_CHIRP_MASS and 30+ related
    constants).

  - Multi-mode HMNS oscillation with frequencies and amplitudes from numerical
    relativity literature (NSMERGER_HMNS_F2_FREQ_KHZ,
    NSMERGER_HMNS_FSPIRAL_FREQ_KHZ, etc.).

  - MRI spiral pitch for disk density waves.

  - Two-component kilonova with separate density profiles, velocities, and
    r-process heating decay.


RENDERING PIPELINE
------------------

  - CPU (Python) computes orbital state, phase progress, and ~50 physics
    parameters per frame, packing them into a 256-byte MergerParams uniform.

  - GPU compute shader (renderer/src/compute/merger.wgsl) scatters 1.16 million
    particles across 9 physical layers in a single dispatch — star cores, GW
    rings, disk, jets, dynamical ejecta, wind ejecta, corona, tidal arms,
    remnant glow. Each particle's position is computed procedurally from its
    layer's distribution, using PCG hashing for deterministic randomness
    without storing per-particle state.

  - CPU mmap writes 5k ambient deep-space stars (the only layer the GPU does
    not generate procedurally).

  - 48 FPS in this capture run — slightly below the 60 FPS target. The disk
    layer (400k of the 1.16M) is the heaviest; cutting to 800k total particles
    would hit 60.
